**A non-owner can no longer rebind an UNOWNED child standard under an OWNED governed ancestor and opt that subtree out of the ancestor's governance (#4499, CWE-863; local and federated, both backends).** The shared namespace-standard bind verdict (`ns_standard_ancestor::set_admission`) consulted the governing ancestor only for a first bind or a severed repair. A rebind was decided by the #3758 owner check alone, and #3758 lets anyone rebind an unowned standard (owner absent, empty or `system`: a legacy, system-stamped or operator-bound standard). So a principal who owned neither the ancestor nor the child could replace such a child standard with a permissive one. That opted the subtree out of the ancestor's write, promote, delete and approval-depth gates, through the HTTP, MCP and SAL funnels and, after #4495, the federated `namespace_meta[]` apply. A rebind of an unowned standard now runs the same ancestor-owner check as a first bind: under an owned governing ancestor only that ancestor's owner (or an admin) may rebind it, and under a severed governing ancestor the rebind fails closed. The #3758 unowned-PASS still holds where no owned governing ancestor exists. A rebind of an owned standard is unchanged, and stays the current owner's call. Refusals use the existing closed 403 `NOT_OWNER` shape on HTTP, the same text on MCP and SAL, and the federated per-entry skip.
