**The doc symbol-anchor gate counts a prose `<` as a group opener only when a type provably follows it, and refuses an anchor whose verdict depends on any other `<` (#5608).** The comparison rule needed whitespace right before the `<`, so a `<` written in a code span, in quotes or in parentheses, or followed by a digit or a minus, opened a phantom group that absorbed a real over-closer after the anchor. A closed code span before the anchor is now atomic text and never opens a group. Outside spans, a `<` followed by an identifier, a lifetime, `&`, `(`, `[`, `*`, `<`, `/`, `:` or `?` opens a group; any other `<` may or may not, and when the anchor is judged differently under the two counts it is refused as `UNDECIDABLE_LT`, cited as written. Behaviour change: a word-attached `<` followed by whitespace (`Vec< src/x.rs::T<U>>`, the #5530 and #5531 greens) is now refused rather than accepted. Not modelled: whether a `<` that is followed by a type is really a comparison (`a <b`) is not decided; it counts as an opener. Base: accepted; tip before this fix: accepted; now reported or refused. Real tree: 0 lines change. The `--self-test` gains 16 cases (#5608).
