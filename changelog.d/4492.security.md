**A principal can no longer make another principal's governed subtree refuse every governed operation by hanging an over-depth parent chain above it (#4492, option 1; CWE-400 / CWE-863).** Since #4477, a governance chain whose entitled explicit `parent_namespace` walk exceeds `MAX_NAMESPACE_DEPTH` refuses the governed operation instead of being truncated. Nothing at bind time prevented building such a chain, though. A principal who could bind an unbound root segment could link a same-owner chain of nine or more parents above it. Every governed write, promote, delete and reflect under that root then returned 500, including those in another principal's governed descendant such as `root/proj`. A namespace-standard bind is now refused at bind time when it would push an entitled explicit chain past the bound. The refusal is a typed 400, the same fixed, value-free text on HTTP, MCP and the SAL trait (`InvalidInput`), and no row is written. 400 matches the existing bind-link refusals, such as a namespace declared as its own parent: the requested link is invalid input, not a state conflict. The check covers every chain the bind changes: the bound namespace's own chain, chains extended at their top, chains below it (slash roots and explicit-parent children whose walk reaches it), and a rebind that changes a node's owner and so changes which hops are entitled. It runs on every bind funnel, including the federated `namespace_meta[]` apply on both receive loops and the postgres admin apply context. It runs inside the bind's write transaction, under the sqlite writer lock and the postgres bind advisory lock, so two binds that are each legal alone cannot together exceed the bound. A bind that keeps or shortens a pre-existing over-depth chain is allowed, so an operator can always repair one. The decision is one pure function (`governance::bind_chain_depth`) shared by both backends. Not in this change (the remainder of #4492, after the approval-gate unit): the descendant-side first-bind twin, and a `doctor` finding that lists pre-existing over-depth chains (which still refuse at resolution, per #4477).
