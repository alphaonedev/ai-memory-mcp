**A governed ancestor now governs every descendant on postgres, not just the five nearest levels, and an over-deep governance chain is refused instead of silently truncated (#4477, CWE-863; sqlite/postgres parity).** The postgres governance chain kept only the 5 most-specific `/` levels and followed at most 5 explicit `parent_namespace` hops (`GOVERNANCE_INHERITANCE_DEPTH_CAP`, F-A2A1.2), so a standard more than five levels above a namespace governed nothing there. Every postgres resolver walks that chain: the write, promote and delete gates, the reflect approval gate (`require_approval_above_depth`) and the #4356 ancestor-owner bind gate. A stranger's depth-1 reflect into a depth-7 descendant of a depth-gated root was therefore APPLIED on postgres and PENDING on sqlite. Both backends also silently dropped any explicit parent beyond their hop budget (postgres 5, sqlite 8), a fail-open loss of a governance layer. Now one rule, `governance::chain_depth`, applies on both backends, identical for both walks. The chain is complete: no `/` level and no entitled explicit parent is dropped. Each walk is bounded by `MAX_NAMESPACE_DEPTH` (8), and a walk that would exceed it refuses the governed operation, never resolving against a truncated chain. The deliberate stops are unchanged: the global `*`, a cycle, and the #2542 unentitled cross-tenant graft. Behaviour change on sqlite: an explicit-parent chain longer than 8 hops above a governed ancestor used to resolve without that ancestor and now refuses. The two postgres chain builders (pool and transaction) are now one builder in `store/postgres/governance_chain_4477.rs`, and `postgres.rs` shrinks by about 210 lines. `GOVERNANCE_INHERITANCE_DEPTH_CAP` keeps its name and now equals `MAX_NAMESPACE_DEPTH` as a refusal bound. The display-only LOOKUP chain keeps its existing lenient fallback on sqlite.
