A widen the kernel refuses is reported once, with its remedy, and leaves no journal line behind (#6023, #6028). The widen step now tracks whether its chmod may have landed: a widen refused before its chmod, or one whose chmod failed and whose inode is then read back to carry exactly the mode the walk found (#6035), left that mode in place, so the janitor releases its journal line and reports the one failure instead of trying to put a mode back and reporting a second, "not known" mode. On Linux an `EPERM` from that chmod - the case of an append-only or immutable inode behind a mode that hides its flags - names the `chattr -a` / `chattr -i` remedy. On macOS the widen step re-reads the entry before its journal line is written, so a widen it refuses there (a second link, a replaced entry) leaves no `+` line claiming a widen that never happened (#6028).
