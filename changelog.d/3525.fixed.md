### Fixed

- **[performance][postgres] A contended `PostgresStore::connect` no longer pays a
  full 25 ms backoff step before its first re-probe of the migration advisory
  lock** ([#3525](https://github.com/alphaonedev/ai-memory-mcp/issues/3525)).
  [#3519](https://github.com/alphaonedev/ai-memory-mcp/issues/3519) replaced a
  blocking `pg_advisory_lock` wait — which pins a transaction snapshot and
  deadlocks the holder's `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` — with a poll of short
  `pg_try_advisory_lock` probes. That fix is correct and is not touched here.
  What it also did, unintentionally, was make every contended acquire wait out a
  flat 25 ms first step, including the overwhelmingly common case of a peer whose
  ladder is a no-op re-read of `schema_version` and releases in single-digit
  milliseconds.
  - Measured on the certified tier (160 connects at 4-way concurrency against an
    already-migrated database, 3 runs per revision, interleaved, idle host):
    median per-connect latency rose from **398.7 ms** before #3519 to
    **503.0 ms** after (+104.3 ms, +26.2 %), and p90 from **470.0 ms** to
    **744.6 ms** (+274.6 ms, +58.4 %). The distributions do not overlap at any
    percentile. The mechanism matches the ladder exactly: a waiter behind a
    ~400 ms holder probes at 25/75/175/375/775 ms and wakes at 775.
  - **The fix the issue proposed does NOT work, and was measured before it was
    discarded.** Prepending a 5 ms sleep to a doubling ladder does not change
    its coarse steps, it SHIFTS them all 5 ms later (cumulative probes
    5/30/80/180/380 versus 25/75/175/375), so a waiter that becomes eligible at
    the measured ~55 ms lock hold wakes at 80 ms instead of 75 ms — marginally
    WORSE. The re-measure confirmed it: p90 746.5 ms against the unfixed
    744.6 ms, i.e. unchanged. The binding constraint was never the first step
    but the RATIO between consecutive probes, because that ratio bounds how far
    past the actual release a waiter can sleep.
  - The schedule is therefore tied to the elapsed wait —
    `sleep = clamp(elapsed / 8, 5 ms, 1000 ms)` — which bounds the overshoot at
    ~12.5 % of the time already waited instead of the doubling ladder's ~100 %.
    The first probe remains immediate, so an uncontended connect still never
    sleeps at all, and the steady state is unchanged: once a wait passes 8 s the
    gap is EXACTLY the #3519 cap of one probe per second.
  - **Measured after the fix** (same harness, same host, three runs per
    revision): median per-connect p50 **421.3 ms** and p90 **522.8 ms**, against
    a 6e92e93d baseline of 410.3 / 474.9 — closing **89 %** of the p50
    regression and **83 %** of the p90. A residual of +11 ms (+2.7 %) at the
    median and +48 ms (+10.1 %) at p90 REMAINS and is not claimed to be zero:
    it is the irreducible cost of polling rather than being woken by the lock
    manager, it compounds across queued waiters, and the blocking wait that
    would remove it is the deadlock #3519 exists to prevent.
  - The probe budget is stated honestly rather than flatteringly: the ramp
    reaches the cap in 54 probes at ~8.25 s, and a waiter riding the FULL
    1,800 s budget issues ~1,845 probes — not the ~100 an "it's only the ramp"
    reading suggests. #3519's ladder issues ~1,804 over the same budget, so the
    tighter wake-up costs ~41 extra probes across a thirty-minute wait (+2.3 %),
    each an uncontended shared-memory lock-table check. A unit test pins both
    totals and the difference.
  - **The bounded fail-closed wait is unchanged.** `MIGRATION_LOCK_WAIT_TIMEOUT_MS`,
    the `min(remaining)` clamp that stops a sleep overrunning the deadline, and
    the refusal text all keep their #3519 values and behaviour. Only *when*
    inside that budget a waiter looks has changed, never how long it is willing
    to wait before refusing.
  - The schedule moved out of the acquire loop into
    `src/store/pg_migration_lock.rs` as the pure function
    `migration_lock_probe_delay_ms`, so it is data pinned by unit tests rather
    than literals: the short-but-never-zero first re-probe (a zero delay would be
    a spin against the database a peer is migrating), the overshoot bound,
    monotonicity and the cap, the probe budget against #3519's, and the
    unchanged steady state.
  - While measuring this, the pre-#3519 deadlock was reproduced live on the
    certified tier at only **four** concurrent connects (the original report used
    eight): three waiters parked in `pg_advisory_lock` holding snapshots, the
    holder's `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY idx_archived_ns_archived_at` waiting on
    `virtualxid` for exactly those snapshots, no progress in 16 minutes, and no
    PostgreSQL deadlock-detector fire because the cycle closes through the
    application. #3519's poll remains load-bearing.
