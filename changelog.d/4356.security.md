**A non-owner can no longer opt a governed subtree out of its ancestor's governance by binding the first child standard (#4356, CWE-284).** The namespace-standard bind gates (#929, #2541, #2542, #3758) judged the memory being bound, the declared parent and the standard currently bound to the target, but never the ancestor chain of a target with no binding of its own. Any caller could therefore bind a memory they own as the standard of `<governed>/<child>` with a permissive policy; the child's own policy then won the most-specific-wins walk and the ancestor's write, promote, delete and approval gates (including `require_approval_above_depth`, whose omitted key stops the walk under leaf-first-wins) stopped applying in that subtree, on both backends. Binding the first standard, or repairing a severed binding (the #3758 SET repair path), under a governed ancestor now requires the owner of the nearest governing ancestor's standard: the nearest level on the governance chain (the `/` hierarchy plus entitled explicit parents) whose standard carries a policy; a bound standard with no policy does not shadow a governed grandparent. The verdict is one backend-blind predicate, `ns_standard_ancestor::set_admission`, fed by a per-backend reader that classifies the stored metadata raw: an ancestor whose metadata is not a JSON object, is not JSON at all, or whose `governance` blob does not deserialize (the #4285 corrupt shape) is treated as severed and fails closed, as does a dangling or NULL pointer and any read fault. The MCP, HTTP and SAL funnels all run it, inside the bind's own write transaction (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on sqlite; a transaction-scoped advisory lock on postgres), so a child bind racing an in-flight ancestor bind cannot pass on a stale chain. Refusals use the existing 403 `NOT_OWNER` shape on both backends and never name the owner or echo the policy. Unaffected: an ungoverned root, an unowned (`system` / absent owner) ancestor standard, the daemon principal and admin contexts (including the operator CLI `namespace set-standard` and `import`, which are operator-only). Not covered by this fix (follow-ups): the global `*` standard is not a governing ancestor; the federated `namespace_meta` lanes keep their #2479 / #2536 peer-scope gate (they replicate binds authorized on the origin node); an ancestor owner cannot delegate child binds without operator bypass. Residuals named: child standards bound by a non-owner BEFORE this fix keep their opt-out until an operator reviews them (doctor follow-up #4368); and a child bound before its ancestor's first bind (in time, or by losing the serialised race to it) legitimately keeps its own standard, as before. Decided by 5-agent vote (4d3ea1c5), unanimous option A.
