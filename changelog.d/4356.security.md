**A non-owner can no longer opt a governed subtree out of its ancestor's governance by binding the first child standard (#4356, CWE-284).** The namespace-standard bind gates (#929, #2541, #2542, #3758) judged the memory being bound, the declared parent and the standard currently bound to the target, but never the ancestor chain of a target with no binding of its own. Any caller could therefore bind a memory they own as the standard of `<governed>/<child>` with a permissive policy; the child's own policy then won the most-specific-wins walk and the ancestor's write, promote, delete and approval gates stopped applying in that subtree, on both backends. Binding the first standard, or repairing a severed binding, under a governed ancestor now requires the owner of the nearest governing ancestor's standard (a bound standard with no governance policy does not shadow a governed grandparent). The verdict is one backend-blind predicate, `ns_standard_ancestor::set_admission`, fed by a per-backend reader; the MCP, HTTP, CLI and SAL funnels all run it and both SAL adapters re-run it inside the bind transaction as the fail-closed floor, refusing with the existing 403 `NOT_OWNER` and never naming the owner. An ungoverned root, an unowned ancestor standard, the daemon principal and admin contexts are unaffected, a severed governing ancestor fails closed, and a read fault refuses. Not covered by this fix (follow-ups): the global `*` standard is not a governing ancestor, the federated `namespace_meta` lanes keep their #2479 peer-scope gate, and an ancestor owner cannot delegate child binds without operator bypass. Decided by 5-agent vote (4d3ea1c5), unanimous option A.
