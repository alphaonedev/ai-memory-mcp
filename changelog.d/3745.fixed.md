- **[data-integrity] A webhook delivery could sit unstarted behind a sibling
  delivery's whole retry ladder, and its DLQ row with it**
  ([#3745](https://github.com/alphaonedev/ai-memory-mcp/issues/3745),
  [#3940](https://github.com/alphaonedev/ai-memory-mcp/issues/3940)).
  `Cargo.lock` pinned tokio 1.52.0, the one release whose `spawn_blocking`
  pool could decide "an idle thread will take it" without reserving that
  thread. Two blocking tasks submitted while one thread was idle then shared
  that single thread, and the second waited until the first returned
  (upstream tokio#8056, "causes `spawn_blocking` to hang", reverted in 1.52.1
  by tokio#8057). Each webhook delivery runs on a blocking thread, and a
  failed one holds it for up to ~26 s of ACK timeouts and backoff. A second
  delivery, or a refused one whose DLQ write takes milliseconds, could
  therefore start only after that ladder ended. If the daemon or a one-shot
  CLI stopped first, the queued delivery never wrote its audit row or its
  DLQ row. The same stall made the #3711 and k7 DLQ test cells red
  intermittently: their poll loop was itself a long `spawn_blocking` task
  that took the only thread. The futex parking seen in #3745's thread dump
  was ordinary idle-thread and `reqwest::blocking` waiting, not a lock in the
  dispatcher.
  - tokio is now locked at 1.53.1, and `Cargo.toml` requires at least 1.52.1
    for both the runtime and dev dependency, so the broken release cannot be
    resolved again.
  - `tests/spawn_blocking_no_starvation_3745.rs` pins the pool contract
    through the tokio the crate links: exactly one idle thread, two
    back-to-back `spawn_blocking` calls, and the first task can finish only
    after the second has run. On 1.52.0 the cell fails on its first starved
    trial (about 55 % of trials starve). On 1.53.1 it passes every trial.
