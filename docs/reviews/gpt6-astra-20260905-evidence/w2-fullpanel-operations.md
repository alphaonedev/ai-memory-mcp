## Challenges accepted

**A: A second canonical get is a practical mitigation, not a cure for fabricated version metadata.** Accepted. The assessment correctly acknowledges CAS prevents the demonstrated stale write. It does not infer a lost update. Mission contracts should minimize avoidable retries because swarms multiply the extra call/cost and provenance ambiguity.

**B: Signed state and authorized state are not identical.** Accepted. A signed relationship does not authenticate endpoint content; source-read permission and admin membership must hold on the actual exposed route. The narrowed local-MCP findings explicitly do not establish PostgreSQL/HTTP exploitation. Operations must certify the served posture, not merely install the strongest available profile.

**C: Native PostgreSQL, actual replay and historical federation acceptance materially strengthen the positive case.** Accepted. Read-only SQL success is not application failover, but it disproves an absent/fake-tier allegation. The report credits historical cross-host and real-agent work while requiring current receipt and recovery contracts. An honest local-only 202 receipt is valuable, and permanent loss of its only disk cannot fairly be scored as violating a quorum guarantee it never made.

**D: Daemon restart and agent business continuation are separate.** Accepted. The traceable 333/333 result is real retention evidence and must remain credited. Lost unacknowledged outcomes, model restart, adapter capture receipt, downstream external effects and dependency readiness are different tests. The draft's five clocks and durable-effect ledger are the right expansion. Requiring capture receipts does not require preserving model hidden state.

**E: Utility is not universal superiority, and task-level randomization must respect shared-memory interference.** Accepted. Complete mission/isolated swarm allocation, retention-needed versus matched-information comparisons, mechanism ablations and independent outcome oracles avoid unfair or contaminated experiments. Native storage cannot decide whether a true historical statement applies to the current business case. Source/key/model diversity alone cannot establish independent evidence.

**G: Convenience export is distinct from native backup; large-module refactoring is not a substitute for correctness.** Accepted. The report preserves portability_complete:false and confines snapshot/accounting limitations to the actual export implementation. Recovery should use the declared native backup contract. Refactoring is justified by contract ownership and regression evidence, not by a cosmetic line-count target.
